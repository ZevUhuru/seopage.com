# SEO Page Stances

How we choose the psychological posture a page takes toward its buyer, from
evidence rather than taste. A **stance** is the relationship the page assumes
with the reader in its first ten seconds: what it claims they are feeling, and
what it therefore says first.

One page, one stance. A page that takes two stances persuades nobody, because
the visitor cannot tell what it thinks of them.

> copy kept beside the pages it governs, so the builder's prompts and the page

## Why this exists

Both pages we have built to a high standard were built the same way, and we
only noticed afterwards:

1. Find out, from the buyer's own words, what they are actually afraid of.
2. Pick the one posture that speaks to that fear without lying.
3. Build every section to serve it.

What differed was the posture, and the posture came from the evidence, not from
preference. SEOPage's own page takes **The Alarm** because a plumber genuinely
does not know an AI is naming a competitor. Access Evolved takes **The Missing
Person** because his buyer's problem is a person who left, not a feature that
is absent. Neither would work on the other's page.

This document turns that into a procedure, so the choice takes an hour instead
of a week, and so it can eventually be an intake field rather than a judgment.

---

## The selection procedure

Five observations, in this order. Each is cheap, and together they usually
name the stance before you have written a word.

**1. Read their own page's order of claims.** What does their H1 put first?
Access Evolved leads "Support & Modernization", support first. That ordering is
the business telling you what it thinks it sells, and a page that contradicts it
is asking the client to change businesses, which is a different conversation.

**2. Count their proof.** Which kind of engagement do their case studies
actually describe? Access Evolved leads with support but five of six case
studies are rebuild projects. Support is the door; projects are the revenue.
The stance should open the door they already use and qualify toward the work
they actually sell.

**3. Harvest the buyer's vocabulary.** Not their industry's vocabulary, theirs.
Go where they post: Microsoft Q&A, manufacturer forums, trade boards, review
sites. Collect verbatim sentences. If every third thread is organized around a
person who left, that is the stance, and it was not your idea.

**4. Read the SERP's stance, not its content.** What posture does every ranking
page already take? In the Access market, ten of eleven commercial pages hide
pricing behind "contact us" and open with a free consultation. The unoccupied
posture is usually the one with an edge, provided the client can honor it.

**5. Check the client's operating capacity.** A stance is a promise. The
Emergency Desk promises someone answers the phone. If the client is one person
with client work, that page is worse than no page. **Never select a stance the
client cannot physically keep.**

## The stances

Each has: when it applies, the signal that selects it, the move it makes, what
it demands of the client, and how it fails.

### 1. The Missing Person

> The thing that runs your business was built by someone who is gone.

- **Select when** the buyer inherited a critical asset they do not understand,
  and the risk is concentrated in a person rather than a technology.
- **Signal** their vocabulary is organized around an absence: "a co-worker who
  no longer works here", "the only person who knew it". Systems older than the
  staff operating them.
- **The move** name the situation more precisely than they expected, then make
  the obvious question ("who takes this over?") the service.
- **Demands** a credible taker-over: a named human, a documented method, and a
  first engagement that leaves them better off even if they never come back.
- **Fails when** the builder is still employed, or the page slides into
  shaming the client for the state of the thing.
- **Worked example** Access Evolved V1.

### 2. The Alarm

> Right now, something is choosing someone else, and you cannot see it.

- **Select when** discovery is intermediated, so the buyer loses business
  invisibly: AI answers, search, marketplaces, aggregator apps.
- **Signal** the buyer cannot observe the loss without a tool they do not own.
  Competitors are chosen in a room the buyer is not in.
- **The move** show the invisible room, then show them in it.
- **Demands** the ability to *demonstrate* the loss. Without a live
  demonstration it reads as fear-selling.
- **Fails when** the alarm is unverifiable, over-repeated, or the page cannot
  resolve it in the same scroll.
- **Worked example** the seopage.com homepage.

### 3. The Emergency Desk

> It is broken now. Start here.

- **Select when** demand is acute, transactional and searched by symptom, and
  the buyer's process is stopped today.
- **Signal** the SERP's high-intent queries are error strings and failures:
  "won't open", "corrupted", specific error numbers.
- **The move** triage before sale. Diagnose the symptom on the page, for free,
  before asking for anything.
- **Demands** *real responsiveness*. A published number that goes to voicemail
  destroys more trust than a slow page ever would.
- **Fails when** the client cannot answer, or the page converts an outage into
  a project pitch while the buyer is still on fire.
- **Worked example** Access Evolved V2, held back pending his capacity.

### 4. The Honest Arithmetic

> Here is the number, including the part that argues against us.

- **Select when** the category hides pricing, the buyer fears being upsold, and
  the purchase has a recurring cost tail.
- **Signal** competitors uniformly refuse to publish numbers; buyer forums are
  full of cost-shock stories after the fact.
- **The move** publish the arithmetic, including the case for not buying, and
  let it qualify people out.
- **Demands** the client actually has numbers, and the nerve to lose the
  buyers the maths disqualifies.
- **Fails when** the numbers are invented, or the honesty is performed rather
  than real ("transparent pricing: contact us").
- **Worked example** Access Evolved V3, the Power Apps licensing calculator.

### 5. The Rumor Correction

> What you were told is not true, and acting on it is expensive.

- **Select when** the market is moving on a false belief, and the client's work
  is often bought or avoided because of it.
- **Signal** high search demand on "is X dying / discontinued / being replaced",
  and experts in the field visibly contemptuous of the claim.
- **The move** correct the record with the vendor's own dates, which buys
  authority, then reframe the project from replace to repair.
- **Demands** citations from the primary source. This stance dies on a
  paraphrase.
- **Fails when** the correction is itself spun, or when the client's revenue
  actually depends on the rumor being true.

### 6. The Named Human

> A person, not a firm, is going to answer.

- **Select when** the buyer is handing over something they cannot afford to
  lose, and the competition is faceless: offshore shops, marketplaces, large
  partners.
- **Signal** the category's boutiques all differentiate on a named founder,
  while the large players show logos and headcounts.
- **The move** put the person, their credential and their direct line above the
  company. Scale is the competitor's story; accountability is ours.
- **Demands** a real person willing to be the face and to pick up.
- **Fails when** the client wants to look bigger than they are, which is the
  instinct to argue them out of.

### 7. Receipts First

> Here is the unedited evidence, before any claim.

- **Select when** the category's claims are unverifiable and the buyer has been
  burned, or when our own result is genuinely unusual.
- **Signal** competitor pages full of percentages with no methodology.
- **The move** lead with the raw artifact: the screenshot, the dashboard, the
  invoice, dated and unretouched, including the numbers that went down.
- **Demands** real evidence we are allowed to publish.
- **Fails when** the receipt is cropped to flatter, which a buyer spots
  instantly and never forgives.
- **Worked example** the Ahrefs screenshots on seopage.com, red numbers left in.

### 8. The Threshold Offer

> Buy the diagnosis, not the project.

- **Select when** the real engagement is high-ticket, scoping is genuinely hard,
  and the buyer's fear is being quoted a number that triples.
- **Signal** buyer forums contain stories of runaway invoices; competitors sell
  "free consultations" that are sales calls.
- **The move** make the first purchase a small, fixed, useful thing that stands
  alone. They keep the output either way.
- **Demands** a deliverable worth having on its own.
- **Fails when** the threshold is a disguised sales call, which the buyer can
  smell.

## Cross-cutting rules

These apply whatever stance is selected.

**The Mirror.** People and imagery on the page depict the buyer, not the
client's aspiration and not a stock ideal. Cast from the ICP's real
demographics, setting and clothing. The clay plumber suits a plumber; a fifty-
something operations manager in a plant office suits a manufacturer. Getting
this wrong is the fastest way to lose a page that is otherwise right.

**A stance must exclude someone.** If no visitor reads the headline and leaves,
it is not doing any work. Qualification is the point.

**Nothing invented.** No statistic without a traceable primary source, no
number we cannot show. Vendor-sponsored figures are named as such or dropped.
The widely-quoted ones are usually the worst: the "video lifts conversion 80%"
claim is one 2012 vendor white paper about a single client.

**Stance versus keyword is a real tension, and it is Zev's call.** The most
persuasive headline often will not contain the target term. The options are to
fold the term in, or to carry it in the eyebrow, title and H2s while the H1
does the persuading. Never silently drop the term to get a nicer sentence.

**Anti-stances, which never ship:** manufactured deadlines; claiming a platform
is dying when it is not; shaming the buyer for the state of their system;
borrowing fear the client cannot resolve on the same page.

## Using it in the builder

Today this is a checklist a human runs. The path to it being a field:

1. **Intake asks what the page must do**, per `INTAKE_SPEAKS_OUTCOMES`, and the
   research step collects the five observations above.
2. **The stance is selected and recorded on the artifact**, with the evidence
   that chose it. A page whose stance nobody can justify is a page nobody can
   review.
3. **The stance selects prompt variants** for headline, section order and proof
   requirements, rather than the copy model inventing a posture per run.
4. **The stance is what an A/B test tests.** Two headlines inside one stance is
   a small experiment; two stances is the real one.

The end state Zev is after: see the customer, read five signals, name the
stance. This document is the lookup table that makes that possible, and it
should grow a row every time a client does not fit an existing one.
